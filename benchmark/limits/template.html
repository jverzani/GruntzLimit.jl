Symbolic Limits Comparison

GruntzLimit on three term types (Julia Expr, SymEngine and SymbolicUtils) and SciML's SymbolicLimits.jl on SymbolicUtils terms, on a set of limits chosen to be hard: the worked examples of Gruntz's thesis, cancellations that defeat numerical evaluation, high-order series at finite points, one-sided limits, and limits with the parameters a and b. Each answer is compared with a known value.

What stands out

GruntzLimit solves all 66 on every term type (Expr, SymEngine and SymbolicUtils), in under 3 s in all from a cold start: all of Gruntz's §8 examples, the nested-radical, high-order-series (−1/30 for (sin∘tan − tan∘sin)/x⁷) and parameter problems, the limit points π/4 and π/2, and every one-sided limit.

Changes since the first run (same day, same problems): GruntzLimit now evaluates sin and cos at rational multiples of π with denominators 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6, so cos(π/2) is zero. Before, it returned 0 for (π/2 − x)·tan x at π/2 (problem 57, limit 1) and an expression dividing by cos(π/2) for tan(x)tan 2x at π/4 (56). A leading coefficient free of x that evaluates to zero is now passed over instead of trusted. Comparable exponentials whose exponents have an irrational ratio, such as 3ˣ and 5ˣ, are now handled, which solves Gruntz 8.5 and (3ˣ + 5ˣ)1/x (5, 12). And Expr input now accepts rational literals such as 5//7 (Gruntz 8.15, problem 15). Last, an infinite limit whose sign depends on a parameter, such as a·log x, is no longer an error where only its being infinite matters, which solves xᵃ/eˣ (38); where the sign matters, as in xa−2, it still is.

SymbolicLimits supports only +, *, integer powers, exp and log, so on the same terms it does 19: 46 problems stop with “Not implemented” (sqrt, sin, tan, a power with a non-integer exponent) or with a MethodError for a symbolic limit point (π/4, π/2, a). With powers and radicals rewritten as exp and log first it does 37, among them Gruntz 8.1–8.4, 8.6–8.8, 8.10, 8.14, 8.19, 8.20 and the parameter problems at ∞. Some still stop on a non-integer power that its own rewriting creates (e.g. Gruntz 8.9, 8.11, 8.16, 8.17), and the 20 problems with trigonometric functions remain out of reach.

SymbolicLimits gives one wrong answer, ∞ for Gruntz 8.18, whose limit is 2, in both rows. Several of its correct answers (problems 3, 4, 23, 50) come with the assumption false in the set it returns; by its own contract the answer is guaranteed only when every assumption holds.

Every problem either SymbolicLimits row solves, GruntzLimit solves too. SymbolicLimits takes 24–29 s in all, mostly on the nested exponentials of Gruntz 8.6, 8.7 and 8.14 (4–5 s each); GruntzLimit takes under 3 s on any term type.

How this was run

A method has an answer when it returned a value free of x and threw nothing; GruntzLimit throws a GruntzError when it cannot compute a limit, SymbolicLimits an ErrorException or ArgumentError. An answer is correct when its value, with a = 1.7 and b = 2.3, matches the known limit to a relative 1e-8 (infinite limits must match exactly). SymbolicLimits also returns the assumptions its zero test made; any other than true are shown with its answer.

Every method gets the same expression. For SymbolicUtils, π is the exact term identity(π) and ℯ is exp(1) with a constant 1, as in GruntzLimit's tests; SymbolicLimits gets exactly the terms GruntzLimit does. The row “powers as exp/log” first rewrites sqrt(u), cbrt(u) and u^v (v not an integer) as exp(log(u)/2), exp(log(u)/3) and exp(v·log(u)), since SymbolicLimits supports only +, *, integer powers, exp and log.

Each method ran in its own Julia process, one after another, with a limit of s per problem; after a timeout the process was restarted at the next problem. Solving time is the sum of the per-problem times from a cold start, so it includes compiling. Load time is the time to load the packages.
