    How it works#

    This is an outline of Gruntz's algorithm as implemented here. Gruntz's thesis has the details and proofs.

    Reduction to \(x \to \infty\)#

    Every limit is reduced to a limit at \(+\infty\):

    • \(x \to -\infty\): substitute \(x \mapsto -x\);

    • \(x \to x_0^+\): substitute \(x \mapsto x_0 + 1/x\);

    • \(x \to x_0^-\): substitute \(x \mapsto x_0 - 1/x\);

    • dir = :both computes both one-sided limits and checks that they agree.

    As \(x \to \infty\), each expression handled here eventually has a constant sign. So abs(u) is replaced by sign(u) * u before the limit is computed.

    Canonical form#

    The input term is converted into an internal canonical form: sums of monomials with exact rational coefficients and exponents, built over atoms. An atom is a variable or constant, an application of exp, log, sin, cos, atan or abs, or a sum used as a base. Some simplifications are applied during conversion:

    • exp(a + c*log(y)) becomes y^c * exp(a), and log of a product splits into a sum;

    • tan, sec, csc, cot and the hyperbolic functions are rewritten in terms of sin, cos and exp;

    • the inverse trigonometric functions are rewritten in terms of atan (for example \(\arcsin u = \arctan\big(u/\sqrt{1-u^2}\big)\)), and the inverse hyperbolic functions in terms of log (for example \(\operatorname{arsinh} u = \log\big(u + \sqrt{u^2+1}\big)\));

    • integer powers of short sums are expanded.

    These rules give the algorithm the zero recognition it needs, without a full computer algebra system.

    The most rapidly varying subexpressions#

    For two functions \(f, g \to \infty\), \(f\) grows faster than \(g\) when \(\log f / \log g \to \infty\). They are in the same comparability class when the ratio tends to a finite nonzero constant. The set \(\Omega\) of most rapidly varying subexpressions of \(f\) is computed recursively. This step itself requires limits, which are computed with the same algorithm.

    If \(x \in \Omega\), the expression is first moved up (\(x \mapsto e^x\)) so that every member of \(\Omega\) is an exponential \(e^{g_i}\).

    Rewriting and series expansion#

    One member \(e^{g_1}\) of \(\Omega\) is chosen, and \(w = e^{\mp g_1} \to 0^+\). Every \(e^{g_i} \in \Omega\) is rewritten as \(w^{-c_i}\, e^{g_i - c_i g_1}\), where \(c_i = \lim g_i / g_1\) is rational. The expression becomes a function of \(w\) whose coefficients are less rapidly varying than \(w\). It is expanded as a generalized power series in \(w\), and the leading term \(c_0 w^{e_0}\) gives the limit:

    • \(e_0 > 0\): the limit is \(0\);

    • \(e_0 < 0\): the limit is \(\pm\infty\), according to the sign of \(c_0\);

    • \(e_0 = 0\): the limit is the limit of \(c_0\), computed recursively (\(c_0\) is simpler).

    When the leading coefficient cancels, the series precision is increased and the expansion is repeated.

    Rebuilding the answer#

    A rational answer is returned as an Int or Rational, and an infinite one as ±Inf. Anything else is converted back from the canonical form by calling build(op, args...) for each compound node, and lift for each number. By default build calls op(args...), except for Expr input, where it constructs an unevaluated call (see Expr answers), and for term types such as SymbolicUtils, where a call on numbers only is built with TermInterface's maketerm so that it stays exact.

    Limitations#

    • Only the functions listed in Supported functions are understood.

    • The limit point is real, and so are all values; complex numbers are rejected.

    • The sign of a constant is decided numerically (in BigFloat), and a constant that is zero to that precision is taken to be zero. Sines and cosines of rational multiples of \(\pi\) with denominators 1, 2, 3, 4 and 6 are evaluated exactly.

    • A free parameter is a real number of unknown sign. When the algorithm needs the sign of a constant that contains one, and the signs given with signs do not decide it, gruntz_limit throws a GruntzError naming the constant. gruntz_cases instead computes the limit for each sign (see Parameters and cases). Where only the size of a limit matters, as when deciding that \(x^a\) and \(x\) are comparable, the sign is not needed.

    • gruntz_limit takes a constant with parameters to be nonzero when that cannot be decided, so an answer such as a/b holds for b != 0. gruntz_cases tests such constants and splits off the case where they vanish.

    • Comparable terms whose exponents have an irrational ratio, such as \(3^x\) and \(5^x\), are handled with irrational powers of \(\omega\); such powers are ordered by the sign of their difference.
